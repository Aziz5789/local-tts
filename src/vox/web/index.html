Writing for speech

These apply to every voice. The models read your text literally, so write it the way it should be said out loud.

Let punctuation set the rhythm

Commas give short pauses, periods give full stops, and question marks lift the ending. Use them where a person would breathe.

End every line with punctuation

Finish with a period, question mark or exclamation mark. A sentence that just stops can sound unfinished or end abruptly.

Keep sentences short

One idea per sentence. Long chains of clauses are where speech starts to rush or lose its shape. Split them up.

Write numbers as words when it matters

Years and prices usually come out right, but times and dates can stumble. Write three thirty, not 3:30.

Spell out abbreviations

Write January instead of Jan. and versus instead of vs. Short forms with periods are easy to misread.

Leave out symbols and markup

Emoji, URLs, hashtags, brackets and bullet points are either skipped or read strangely. Describe them in words if they matter.

Respell tricky names

If a name keeps coming out wrong, spell it how it sounds, like Nguyen as Win. Listen and adjust.

Put feeling in the words

There is no emotion setting. Tone comes from the wording and punctuation, and for cloned voices, from the reference clip.

Cloned voices

Your own voices use Qwen3-TTS 0.6B Base, which copies a voice from its reference clip and transcript.

About a minute and a half per take

Vox caps one generation at roughly 90 seconds of speech; anything past that gets cut off. Split longer scripts and generate them in parts.

The whole text is one take

Line breaks don't split the text. Mark pauses between paragraphs with punctuation instead.

Every take is a little different

The model samples its output, so the same text varies slightly each time. If one take sounds off, generate it again.

Style comes from the clip

This model doesn't follow instructions like say it happily. A calm reference gives calm speech, so record the tone you want.

English only, for now

The model knows ten languages, but Vox currently tells it every text is English. Other languages will sound wrong until that's added.

Expect about 20 seconds

Most of the wait is loading the model, which Vox does on every request to stay within memory. Longer text adds a little more.

Default voice

The built-in voice uses Kokoro. It's fast and doesn't clone, so it's handy for drafts.

Fix pronunciation directly

Write a word in brackets followed by its phonetic spelling in slashes: [Vox](/vˈɑks/). This only works with the default voice; a cloned voice would read the brackets aloud.

Line breaks start a new chunk

Kokoro reads each line separately, so putting paragraphs on their own lines gives clean breaks between them.

Recording a good clip

A cloned voice can only be as good as its reference recording. This matters more than anything you type.

  • 15 to 25 seconds of one person talking naturally.
  • A quiet room. No music, TV, echo or other voices.
  • Close to the microphone, at a steady distance and volume.
  • The tone you want back. Read the way the voice should usually sound.
  • Trim silence and mistakes at the start and end before uploading.
  • Review the transcript word for word, including filler like um. The model reads it alongside the audio.
  • Any format works. Voice Memos, MP3 and even screen recordings are converted automatically.

Troubleshooting

The ending gets cut off End with punctuation. If the text is long, split it into shorter takes.
A word is mispronounced Write numbers and abbreviations out, or respell the word how it sounds.
The voice doesn't sound like them Check the transcript matches the clip exactly, then try a cleaner or longer recording.
Speech sounds flat or robotic Use a more expressive reference clip, and add commas and question marks to the text.
Odd sounds, repeats or mumbling Generate again. If it keeps happening, shorten the text or re-record the reference.

Same thing in the CLI

The web app and the vox command share one voice library, so anything you do in one shows up in the other.

Speak with the default voicevox speak "Hello there." -o hello.wav
Speak with a cloned voicevox speak "Hello there." -e qwen -v aziz -o hello.wav
Add a voicevox voices add clip.m4a --id gavin --name "Gavin"
List voicesvox voices list
Delete a voicevox voices remove gavin
Open this appvox init

Review transcript

Play the clip and make the transcript match it word for word. The cloning model reads both together, so a wrong word here makes every generated line worse.

Delete voice?

Its recording, transcript and settings are deleted permanently, here and in the CLI. This can't be undone.
